2. Creative Engineering SOP (How Assets Get Built)

2.1 The Doctrine

REAL PHOTOS ONLY IN PERFORMANCE CREATIVE. No AI-generated imagery, no generative fill, no smears, no overlays, no stock. The single biggest differentiator in the local feed is that our photos are verifiably real — badge them proudly ("REAL GLACIER INSTALL · SAN ANTONIO"). AI generation is permitted only for clearly-labeled visualizations (e.g., the HQ storefront mockup) and never enters ads, posts, or the site as "real."
  • Deterministic rendering. All canvases are code (Python/Pillow at 2x supersampling, single downscale). Measured layouts, fit-loops, ink-bbox centering. Nothing eyeballed. If a human must judge alignment, the layout is wrong.
  • No diagonals on standalone elements. A tilted strike/slash next to level type always reads crooked. Strikethroughs are horizontal, computed from the text's measured ink bounds with equal overshoot. (Learned the hard way; now law.)
  • Minimal-edit doctrine for photo surgery: when a real photo must change (e.g., phone number on the van wrap), preserve every original element and replace only the unavoidable pixels — inheriting measured perspective, lighting, grain, and softness. Re-authored blocks always read fake.
  • No dead zones. Content fills the frame to measured margins; banners sit flush to footers. Whitespace is designed, never leftover.

2.2 The Four-Render Rule

Every ad ships as FOUR files, hand-laid-out per ratio — never auto-cropped:
① 4:5 (1080×1350 @2x = 2160×2700) — main feed asset · ② 1:1 (2160×2160) — square feed placements · ③ 9:16 (2160×3840) — Stories/Reels, all critical content inside safe zones (~y270–1560 at 1x), quiet brand zone below footer · ④ 1.91:1 (2400×1256) — right column/banner, re-composed as two-column. Export a JPG (q92) alongside every PNG for upload-limit safety. Letting Meta auto-crop a layout is a fireable offense.

2.3 QA Gates (every render, before delivery)

  1. Mark-pixel check (automated): scan the eyebrow band for the mark's red pixels; <150 = MISSING = blocked.
  2. Ink-band geometry check (automated): verify content density in every expected zone (headline, checklist, banner, footer).
  3. Human eyeball pass: alignment, crops, spelling, phone number digits, no clipped text, preview at phone size.
  4. Claims audit: every statement on the canvas must be true and verifiable today, not aspirationally.

2.4 Photo Standard (the library)

  • 27-photo real-job library, cataloged in brand/jobs/INVENTORY.md — every photo graded (A/B/C), grouped by job, tagged for best use (post hero / ad / website / carousel).
  • Enhancement pipeline (only permitted processing): LAB levels stretch (p0.5–p99.6), CLAHE local contrast blended 28%, gray-world white balance at 50% strength, saturation ×1.06, unsharp (1.5, −0.5 @ σ2.0). EDSR ×2 super-resolution for low-res sources. Nothing added, nothing removed, scene untouched.
  • Funnel congruence law: the equipment in an ad photo must match the funnel behind it (AC form = AC imagery; the pump photo cost us this lesson). Non-HVAC work photos live on organic only.
  • Same-job photos post together; never mix jobs in one story.

3. Ad Science — Why Ads Win and Why They Die

3.1 The Only Equation That Matters

CPL = CPM ÷ (CTR × CVR × 1000) — cost per lead is three numbers multiplied. Every "hack" is just an attack on one variable: creative attacks CTR, offer/form attacks CVR, engagement quality attacks CPM (Meta charges less for creative people don't scroll past). You cannot negotiate with this equation; you can only feed it.

3.2 The Value Equation (Hormozi) — applied

Perceived value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice). Our funnel weaponizes all four: dream outcome = certainty against the surprise invoice (not "cold air"); likelihood = real photos + price-in-writing promise; time = "fast callback," same-day language where true; effort = "takes about a minute," numbered 3-step process on the canvas. The exact-price promise IS the Grand Slam Offer — it removes the #1 fear (mystery pricing) at zero marginal cost to us, which is precisely what Hormozi means by raising value without raising price.

3.4 The MrBeast Layer (attention physics, translated to ads)

  • First frame = whole promise. He obsesses over the first seconds and the thumbnail because most viewers never pass them. Ad translation: the creative must communicate WHO + WHAT + WHY-CARE in under 1 second, before any text is "read" — a giant 97°, a rusty-vs-new split, a real tech kneeling. If the hook needs the caption, the hook failed.
  • Promise → payoff integrity. His retention comes from always paying off the thumbnail. Ad translation: the landing experience must pay the ad's promise instantly — our form opens asking exactly what the ad said we'd ask. Congruence is retention.
  • Remove every exit ramp. Each confusing element is where attention leaves. One idea per canvas, one CTA, no decoration.

3.5 Why Ads Die (the autopsy list)

  1. Stock creative — category recognition without trust (see the 3-year competitor zombie ad: hardhat indoors, paper sign, "Contact Us").
  2. No offer — nothing to act on today.
  3. Claim-fatigue — "excellence," "trusted," "best" without proof. Proof shows, never tells.
  4. Headline/creative mismatch — component-shuffled automation (electrical headline on HVAC image).
  5. Funnel incongruence — the click's intent doesn't match the form's questions; completion collapses.
  6. Inaccuracy — one number the viewer can disprove (a 103° claim on a 97° forecast) poisons every other claim.
  7. Fatigue — frequency >2.5–3 with sagging CTR; the auction silently raises prices. Rotate every 2–3 weeks.
  8. Founder-itis — panic edits mid-learning; each one resets the algorithm's education and re-buys week one.

4. Copy System (Primary Text · Headlines · Descriptions)

4.1 Primary Text Architecture

Structure: Hook line (stands alone above the fold) → mechanism/agitate (2–3 short paragraphs, air between them) → offer restated as reader's self-interest → contact block stacked:
(866) 665-2210
CallGlacier.com ❄️
Always ship 3–5 text options per ad (one long, one medium, one short) — Meta matches length to reader. Same for headlines and descriptions.

4.2 Headline & Description Rules

  • Headlines ≤ ~40 characters (mobile truncation line). Restate the MECHANISM, not a slogan: "The price in writing. It doesn't move."
  • Descriptions close the loop the headline opens; compress risk-reversal into it where possible ("say no anytime").
  • Write headline/description as pairs even though Meta mixes them.

4.3 Proven Lines (the house arsenal — reuse, remix)

"Your price in writing, before a single tool comes out — and it doesn't move." "No upsell games. No mystery invoices. Just cold air." "Tired systems pick weeks like this to quit." "You're not hiring a logo. You're hiring whoever kneels next to your system." "Every month it limps along, the electric bill collects its pension." "No staging. No stock photos. Just Tuesday." "If the number doesn't work for you, you say no, and we part friends." "Built for Texas heat, run on Texas honesty."

4.4 The Sales Script (NEPQ-style, for lead buyers & homeowners)

Cold open (to HVAC owners, lead-resale side): name the cold call, ask permission for 30 seconds, "I'm not sure we can even help you" disarm, then the leak question: "When somebody searches 'AC repair' at 9 tonight and lands on your website — how many actually call you?" One claim ("one install pays for it"), one ask (15-minute side-by-side), one clean exit ("if yours wins, I'll tell you to keep it"). Two-option close on scheduling, always.

Consumer callback (60 seconds): greet by name → name their stated problem back to them → ONE grading question ("about how old is the system?") → book with a two-option close → repeat the price-in-writing promise. Every ad line is a promise the phone must keep.

5.3 Operating Rhythm

  • Daily (2 min): delivery green? Leads answered <5 min? Nothing else. Do not open the edit panel.
  • 72-hour law: no budget/targeting/creative edits inside any 72h window after a change. Learning resets are the #1 hidden cost in small accounts.
  • Friday council (15 min): per-ad CTR, spend, CPL. Kill: ≥$90 spent + 0 leads + <0.7% CTR. Feed: winner +20% budget per 72h. Never judge replacement-angle ads on week-one CPL (longer cycle, 5–10× ticket).
  • Every 2–3 weeks: one fresh creative into the set (weather strip re-cut, new job-photo ad). Fatigue is a tax; freshness is the write-off.
  • Scaling law: cheap and big don't coexist — expect CPL to rise as daily budget climbs; scale in +20% steps, never doublings.

6.2 The Spread Doctrine

CPL is a vanity metric. SPREAD (sale price − cost per SOLD lead) is the business. A $15 junk lead (volume form, no OTP) sells for ~$20 with refund wars and buyer churn — negative spread. Our $45–55 matured CPL selling at $55–100 exclusive/verified/graded = positive, compounding spread. Never trade product quality for dashboard cosmetics.

6.3 Buyer Pitch & Anchor Defense

Contractors anchor on the $99 service call; re-anchor them on truck-roll economics: $350–600 average repair ticket, ~1-in-3 lead-to-revenue on exclusive fresh leads, replacement conversions worth $3–5k gross each. Market comp: Angi sells the SAME lead to 3–4 competitors at $40–80 each; Google LSA runs $25–90/lead unverified. The line: "Angi would sell that same homeowner to you and three competitors for $60 apiece. I'm giving you the only copy."

6.4 The Money Model Ladder (Hormozi stages, mapped)

  1. Attraction offer: free exact-price estimate (costless, high-certainty, names the fear).
  2. Core offer: repair → replacement upgrade path via honest math (the 1/3 rule).
  3. Continuity: Glacier Club membership (recurring revenue + owned audience + review engine).
  4. Second business: the lead-resale book itself — same machine, wholesale side. Speed + verification + exclusivity is the moat.

8. The SEO Machine

8.1 Technical Layer (built, verified — maintain, don't touch)

  • 40-URL sitemap, trailing slashes matching canonicals exactly (redirect-free), resubmitted on every structural change.
  • Every page: unique title ≤60ch, unique meta description, canonical, single H1, breadcrumbs.
  • Schema: HVACBusiness (@id anchored) + Service (provider by @id reference, never duplicated inline) + FAQPage + BreadcrumbList + Article on posts. NO aggregateRating until real Google reviews exist (fake ratings = manual-action risk). All schema images on own domain.

8.2 Content Flywheel

Blog articles target real buying-intent queries ("AC running but not cooling," "AC replacement cost San Antonio," seasonal checklists). Every article: Article + FAQ schema, branded real-photo cover (doubles as og:image so shares arrive branded), internal links into service + city pages, honest numbers. Cadence: 2–3/month forever beats 12 in January. GSC Performance tab (impressions, position >10) dictates the next topics — Google tells you what it wants to rank you for; feed it exactly that.

8.3 Off-Site Ladder

  1. Citations (wk 1–2): GBP, Bing Places (import from GBP), Apple Business Connect, Yelp, BBB, Angi, Nextdoor, Apple/Bing verified — NAP character-identical everywhere. Bing Webmaster Tools: one-click import from GSC.
  2. Authority links (wk 2–6): Trane dealer locator (manufacturer link = strongest single HVAC link), Chamber, BBB accreditation, ACCA.
  3. Earned local (ongoing): seasonal sponsorships, heat-wave PR pitches with Pro Tips content.

8.4 Timeline Physics (tell the founder the truth)

New domains sandbox for months while trust accrues. Months 1–2: impressions, few clicks. Months 3–6: long-tail wins. Months 6–12: head terms contested — IF links/reviews/content kept stacking. The map pack moves faster than organic (GBP + reviews physics) and IS the money for "near me" queries. Paid is revenue now; SEO is margin later. Anyone promising page one in 30 days is selling a scam or a penalty.
