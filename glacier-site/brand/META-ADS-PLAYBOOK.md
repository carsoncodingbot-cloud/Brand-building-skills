## 1. THE SAN ANTONIO VERDICT (brutally honest)
**Verdict: A- market. Genuinely one of the better HVAC metros in America. Not easy — but structurally rich.**

**Why it's strong:**
- **Climate = recurring revenue.** ~110+ days over 90°F; cooling season runs April–October. AC in SA is life support, not comfort. Systems run near-constantly for half the year → faster wear → more repairs, earlier replacements.
- **Hard water capital.** SA sits on the Edwards Aquifer — some of the hardest water in the US. That kills water heaters and scales plumbing → your plumbing/water-heater line has a built-in villain to market against.
- **Growth + stability.** 2.6M+ metro, one of the fastest-growing large metros; multiple military bases + massive medical sector = recession-resistant homeowner income.
- **Aging housing stock in the core** (1970s–90s neighborhoods = replacement goldmine) alongside newer suburbs (maintenance/club market).

**The honest caveats:**
- **Competition is heavyweight.** SA has some of the largest residential HVAC operators in Texas outspending everyone on TV, radio, and Google. You will NOT outspend them. You out-*character* them (the yeti, the brand, the offer) and out-*speed* them (5-minute callbacks).
- **Summer auction inflation:** June–August CPMs climb because every HVAC company floods the zone. Counter: build audiences in shoulder seasons (March–May, Sept–Oct) when attention is cheap, harvest all year.
- **New-build suburbs** have systems under warranty — target them with club/maintenance, not replacement.

## 2. THE PHILOSOPHY STACK (what we take from each master)
- **Alex Hormozi ($100M Offers/Leads):** The offer beats everything. Value = (Dream outcome × Perceived likelihood) ÷ (Time delay × Effort). ## 4. THE FORM FUNNEL (Meta Instant Forms — exact build)
**Use Meta Instant Forms (Lead Ads), "Higher Intent" type.** Website funnels are for later (when the pixel is seasoned). Instant Forms autofill = lowest friction; "Higher Intent" adds a review screen that filters accidental submits.

**Form: "Glacier Precision Tune-Up — $79" (adapt price to your economics)**
1. **Intro screen:** headline "Claim your $79 Precision Tune-Up (reg. $149)" + 3 bullets: certified techs · takes 60 seconds · we call you within minutes.
2. **Q1 (multiple choice, DISQUALIFIER):** "Do you own the home?" → Own / Rent. *(Renters are ~zero-value HVAC leads; this one question is the single biggest quality filter in the industry.)*
3. **Q2 (multiple choice, ROUTER):** "What best describes your situation?" → Just maintenance / AC acting up / System is 10+ years old / I got a quote from another company. *(This maps every lead to the ladder rung — and flags replacement + second-opinion gold.)*
4. **Q3 (multiple choice, URGENCY):** "When do you want service?" → ASAP / This week / Just planning ahead.
5. **Q4 (short answer):** ZIP code. *(Kills out-of-area waste; SA metro only.)*
6. **Prefill:** Name, phone, email (autofilled by Meta — leave these last).
7. **Review screen** (comes with Higher Intent) → **Thank-you screen:** "We'll call you within minutes — save this number so you know it's us: (866) 665-2210" + button → callglacier.com.

**Form design law:** every added question cuts volume ~10-25% and raises quality. 4 questions + prefill is the sweet spot for home services. Never ask what you don't use.

**CRM wiring (non-negotiable):** Meta leads sitting in the Leads Center = dead leads. Pipe them instantly (Zapier/Make → SMS+email+call task, or LeadsBridge → CRM). Target: **human calls within 5 minutes, 7 days a week.** Script opener (Miner): *"Hey [name], saw you just requested the tune-up special — did I catch you at a bad time?"* (disarming, permission-based).

## 5. TARGETING (2025+ reality)
Meta's algorithm now outperforms manual interest-stacking when fed good creative + conversion signal. **Creative IS the targeting.**
- **Geo:** San Antonio + 25mi radius; exclude far-flung ZIPs you won't drive to. Later: separate campaign for high-value suburbs (Stone Oak, Alamo Heights, Boerne, New Braunfels).
- **Age:** 28–65+. Under ~28 rarely owns SA homes.
- **Detailed targeting:** start **Advantage+ / broad**. If quality is poor after ~50 leads, add ONE homeowner-proxy stack: Home improvement, Home Depot/Lowe's, Nextdoor, HGTV — and let Advantage expand.
- **Exclusions:** create a "Leads last 90 days" custom audience → exclude from acquisition; retarget separately with Club/estimate offers.
- **NOT a Special Ad Category.** HVAC/plumbing services are NOT housing/credit/employment — do not self-select that category or you'll cripple targeting for nothing. (Housing category = property sales/rentals, not home services.)

## 6. CREATIVE SYSTEM (the 3×3 engine)
Always 3 live concepts × fresh iterations; kill the loser weekly, breed the winner.
- **Concept A — Character (Purple Cow):** yeti ad / yeti + AC unit. Goal: stop the scroll, brand recall.
- **Concept B — Native/Documentary (GaryVee):** phone-shot photo or 15s video of a real tech at a real SA house, captioned like a neighbor posted it. These routinely out-perform polished ads on CPL.
- **Concept C — Offer/Direct (Hormozi):** big number ("$79 TUNE-UP"), anchor price crossed out, 3 checkmarks, red CTA. Your existing post templates are this.
- **Hooks (first 3 words matter):** questions from the Miner captions; "$2,000 August" loss-frames; "78°F" curiosity.
- **Format mix:** 1 image (1080×1080), 1 vertical (1080×1920 — Reels/Stories placements are the cheapest CPMs right now), 1 carousel (before/after, 5-sounds).
- **Policy guardrails:** no "your AC is broken" second-person health/attribute implications are fine for HVAC, but avoid: excessive caps, fake countdown scarcity, "guaranteed lowest price" unverifiable claims, before/after implying deception. Clean brand = clean account = the thing you feared never happens.

## 7. CAMPAIGN STRUCTURE + BUDGET (start lean, scale on signal)
```
Campaign 1: LEADS — Tune-Up Offer  (Leads objective, Instant Form)
 └─ Ad set: SA +25mi, broad, all placements ... $30–50/day
     └─ 3 ads (Concepts A/B/C)
Campaign 2 (week 3+): LEADS — Second Opinion ... $15–25/day
Campaign 3 (retargeting, month 2): Club/Estimate to engagers+leads ... $10/day
```
- **Learning phase:** Meta wants ~50 conversions/week/ad set. At $25 CPL that's $180/day ideal — you won't start there. Compromise: one consolidated ad set, don't touch it for 7 days unless spend > 3× target CPL with zero leads.
- **Kill rule:** ad spends 3× target CPL with no lead → off. **Scale rule:** winner holds CPL for 3+ days → +20% budget every 48–72h (never double overnight; resets learning).
- **KPI targets (SA home services, validate against your data):**
  - CPM $15–35 (summer spikes) · CTR ≥ 1.5% (link) · Form conversion 10–20%
  - **CPL: $15–40 tune-up · $30–70 second-opinion · $40–90 replacement-estimate**
  - Lead → booked appointment ≥ 50% (this is a SPEED metric, not an ads metric)
  - Cost per BOOKED JOB < $100 tune-up / < $250 replacement-estimate = winning
- **The only ROI math that matters:** Revenue per lead (incl. ladder: repairs + club + replacements over 90 days) ÷ CPL ≥ 3 → scale mercilessly.

## 9. 90-DAY ROADMAP
- **Weeks 1–2:** Campaign 1 live ($30/day), pixel installed, 5-minute callback drilled. Goal: 30–50 leads, baseline CPL.
- **Weeks 3–4:** Kill losers, add Second Opinion campaign, first iteration of winning creative.
- **Month 2:** Retargeting on, Club offer to lead list, budget to $50–75/day if revenue-per-lead ≥ 3× CPL. Ask every closed customer for a Google review (feeds Meta social proof AND local SEO).
- **Month 3:** Seasonal pivot planning (fall = heating tune-up creative swap — same engine, new skin), lookalike audience off your customer list (1%), evaluate scaling to $100+/day.

## 10. WHAT MAKES ALL OF THIS FAIL (the honest list)
1. Slow callbacks (kills more ROI than any targeting mistake — 21× factor).
2. Judging ads on CPL instead of revenue-per-lead.
3. Touching campaigns daily (learning-phase resets).
4. One ad, run forever (fatigue: frequency > 3 → CPL climbs; refresh creative).
5. Selling "HVAC services" instead of ONE offer per ad.
6. Renter leads (the Own/Rent question is sacred).
7. Summer-only advertising (the cheap leads are bought in spring).
