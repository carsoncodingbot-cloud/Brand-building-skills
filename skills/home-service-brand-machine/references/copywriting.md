# Copy System — Offers, Lines, Scripts

Frameworks credited: Hormozi (Value Equation, Grand Slam Offers), NEPQ-style
questioning (Miner), direct-response structure (Brunson). Everything below
shipped on the reference build.

## 1. The Value Equation (the physics of "yes")

Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort).
Home-service buyers buy certainty against a feared outcome, not a service.
Engineer all four levers on every page and ad:

- **Dream outcome** — name the FEAR, not the service.
- **Likelihood** — verifiable proof: real job photos, real forecast, license.
- **Time delay** — speed language you can keep ("fast callback, often same-day").
- **Effort** — show the tiny process ON the creative: "Takes about a minute.
  We call back fast. Price in writing."

## 2. The fear table (fill per vertical before writing a word)

| Vertical | #1 buyer fear | Grand Slam attraction offer |
|---|---|---|
| HVAC | Mystery invoice / breakdown at peak | Free exact-price estimate, in writing, before a tool comes out — the number doesn't move |
| Plumbing | Small leak becomes catastrophic bill | Free camera/diagnosis, flat written price by symptom class |
| Landscaping | Paying monthly for invisible work | Written scope + photo-proof after every visit |
| Steel buildings | Deposit vanishes / spec creep | Fixed engineered quote with drawings before deposit; milestone payments tied to photographed progress |
| Roofing | Storm-chaser scams / insurance maze | Written scope with drone photos before signing; we meet your adjuster |
| Electrical | Fire risk / being upsold panels | Flat written diagnostic; code issues photographed and explained |

Offer construction rule: the attraction offer costs near-zero marginal
dollars and removes the #1 fear entirely. Discounting is what companies do
when they can't engineer value.

## 3. Voice laws

Established, never "new" (never "newly launched," "we just opened").
Short sentences. Mechanisms over adjectives ("price in writing before work
starts" beats "excellent trustworthy service"). Only offers that exist.
Zero corporate filler ("comprehensive," "solutions," "excellence"). The
reader should hear a competent human, not a brochure. Contractions are fine;
the voice is a straight-talking foreman, not a marketer.

## 4. The line arsenal (remix per vertical, keep the bones)

- FEAR-KILLER: "Your price in writing, before a single tool comes out — and
  it doesn't move."
- ANTI-SLEAZE: "No upsell games. No mystery invoices. Just [outcome]."
- MOMENT: "Tired [systems/pipes/roofs] pick weeks like this to quit."
- HUMAN-TRUST: "You're not hiring a logo. You're hiring whoever kneels next
  to your [system]."
- SMART-MATH: "Every month it limps along, the [utility bill] collects its
  pension."
- AUTHENTICITY: "No staging. No stock photos. Just Tuesday."
- RISK-REVERSAL: "If the number doesn't work for you, you say no, and we
  part friends."
- IDENTITY: "Built for [regional hardship], run on [regional virtue]."

## 5. Page copy architecture (service pages as landing pages)

Every service page is a landing page, structured:

1. **H1** = keyword + fear-killer ("AC Repair in [City] — Exact Prices, In
   Writing").
2. **Pain lead** (2–3 sentences in the buyer's sensory reality: "78° on the
   thermostat and climbing. The air from the vents is room-temperature...").
3. **The promise** — the Grand Slam offer, one paragraph, mechanism explicit.
4. **Embedded funnel** (see `conversion-funnel.md`).
5. **Objection-killers** as scannable feature blocks (speed, written price,
   licensed/insured, real photos of this exact work).
6. **Honest-math section** where relevant (the ⅓ rule: repair > ⅓ of
   replacement cost on an old unit → replace; show both numbers).
7. **FAQ** (schema-backed) answering the questions people actually type.
8. **One CTA band** (never two stacked banners — they compete; one capture
   section + one phone band maximum, differently headlined).

## 6. Ad primary text architecture (3–5 options per ad, always)

Hook line that stands alone above the fold → mechanism/agitation in 2–3
short, air-separated paragraphs → offer restated as the reader's
self-interest → stacked contact block (phone / domain). Ship one long, one
medium, one short — the platform matches length to reader. Headlines ≤ ~40
characters restating MECHANISM, not slogan. Descriptions close the loop and
compress risk-reversal ("say no anytime"). Deliver copy variants in code
blocks so the founder can copy-paste cleanly.

## 7. Scripts (NEPQ posture: calm, curious, permission-based)

**B2B cold (selling leads/partnerships to owners):** name the cold call →
permission for 30 seconds → "not sure we can even help you" disarm → the
leak question ("when someone searches [service] in [city]... how many of
those actually call YOU?") → one claim ("one install pays for it") →
15-minute look with the confidence exit ("if yours wins, I'll tell you to
keep it") → two-option close.

**Consumer callback (60 seconds):** name → mirror their stated problem (use
their form words) → ONE grading question → two-option close on the visit
window → restate the written-price promise. Every published line is a
promise the phone must keep.

**Anchor defense (lead buyers):** "Angi sells that same homeowner to you AND
three competitors for $60 each. I'm giving you the only copy."

## 8. SEO copy rules

Unique titles ≤60ch with city + service + differentiator. Meta descriptions
are ad copy (fear-killer + proof + CTA), not summaries. H1s carry the money
keyword naturally. Blog titles target buying-intent queries: "[symptom]
[city]", "[service] cost [city] [year]", seasonal checklists. Never publish
a number you can't defend (prices as honest ranges with "every home is
different" framing).
